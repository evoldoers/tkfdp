Dear Editors,

Please consider the enclosed manuscript, ``Closed-form Baum--Welch for
TKF-based evolutionary models'', for publication in \emph{Molecular Biology and
Evolution} as a Methods paper.

Fitting the insertion and deletion component of a sequence evolution model has
meant, until now, handing the likelihood to a general-purpose optimizer. We
show that this is unnecessary. For the TKF family of indel models the
expectation-maximization M-step has an exact closed form: the maximum
likelihood insertion rate, deletion rate and fragment extension probability are
functions of five expected counts, which the pair HMM forward-backward
algorithm yields through a score identity on the birth-death-immigration
transition probabilities. No gradient ascent or line search is involved. The
same identity returns endpoint-conditioned counts and dwell times in closed
form for any linear birth-death-immigration process, a result that is not
specific to sequences. Because the sufficient statistics are additive over
alignment columns, a corpus can be pre-aggregated into count tensors, after
which the cost of an EM iteration no longer depends on how much data went into
them; we fit 20,897 Pfam families this way. Applying the same machinery to two
models with latent structure, we find that giving TKF92 a two-component mixture
over fragment lengths buys 1.9 nats per sequence pair of held-out likelihood
for two extra parameters, and improves multiple alignment accuracy on BAliBASE.

Indel models sit underneath sequence alignment, phylogenetic inference and
ancestral reconstruction, but in practice they are usually replaced by fixed
gap penalties, in part because fitting them is awkward. Making the fit exact
and cheap removes that obstacle and puts indel parameters on the same footing
as substitution parameters, which have been fitted by EM for two decades. The
closed-form bridge statistics should be reusable directly by anyone working
with linear birth-death processes, which are common well beyond molecular
evolution.
