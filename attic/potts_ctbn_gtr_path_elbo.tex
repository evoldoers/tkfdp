\begin{center}
{\large\bf Path ELBO for a Potts CTBN under an independent GTR bridge}
\end{center}

Let \(x=(x_1,\ldots,x_L)\in\A^L\), \(|\A|=20\), with Potts target
\[
 \Pi(x)=Z^{-1}e^{-E(x)},\qquad
 E(x)=-\sum_i h_i(x_i)-\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}(x_i,x_j).
\]
Take a reversible one-site GTR proposal
\(R_{ab}=s_{ab}\rho_b\) (\(a\ne b\)), \(s_{ab}=s_{ba}\), and the independent
proposal generator
\[
 \Rcal=\bigoplus_{i=1}^L R .
\]
For \(y=x^{i:b}\) (site \(i\), \(a=x_i\to b\)), use the square-root
Metropolis-symmetric target rate
\[
 \Wcal_{x,y}
 =\Rcal_{x,y}\sqrt{\frac{\Pi(y)\Rcal_{y,x}}{\Pi(x)\Rcal_{x,y}}}
 =s_{ab}\sqrt{\rho_a\rho_b}\,
   e^{-\Delta_i^bE(x)/2},
 \qquad \Delta_i^bE(x)=E(x^{i:b})-E(x).
\]
Write \(r(x)=\sum_{i,b\ne x_i}R_{x_i b}\) and
\(w(x)=\sum_{i,b\ne x_i}\Wcal_{x,x^{i:b}}\).

For one interval of length \(T\), condition the proposal on global endpoints
\(X(0)=A,\ X(T)=B\), giving the independent GTR bridge \(Q_{AB}\).
The path ELBO for the target endpoint likelihood is
\[
\boxed{\;
 \mathcal L_{AB}
 =\log [e^{T\Rcal}]_{AB}
 +\sum_{x,i,b\ne x_i}\bar N^{AB}_{x,i:b}
       \log\frac{\Wcal_{x,x^{i:b}}}{R_{x_i b}}
 -\sum_x\bar T_x^{AB}[w(x)-r(x)] \; }
\]
with
\[
 \log\frac{\Wcal_{x,x^{i:b}}}{R_{ab}}
 =\frac12\left[-\Delta_i^bE(x)+\log\frac{\rho_a}{\rho_b}\right].
\]
Crucially, \(\bar T_x^{AB}\) and \(\bar N^{AB}_{x,i:b}\) are \emph{joint}
Holmes--Rubin statistics of the \(20^L\)-state independent product chain;
they do not factor into products of one-site expected dwell times/counts.

For the spectral form, symmetrize
\[
 H=D_\rho^{1/2}RD_\rho^{-1/2}=U\Lambda U^\top,\qquad
 \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_0,\ldots,\lambda_{19}),
\]
and for \(\boldsymbol r=(r_1,\ldots,r_L)\) define
\[
 \Lambda_{\boldsymbol r}=\sum_i\lambda_{r_i},\qquad
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(x)=\prod_i U_{x_i r_i},\qquad
 Z_{AB}^{(R)}
 =\sum_{\boldsymbol r}\Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(A)\Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(B)
 e^{T\Lambda_{\boldsymbol r}}.
\]
The Holmes--Rubin divided-difference kernel is
\[
 K_T(\alpha,\beta)
 =\int_0^T e^{\alpha t}e^{\beta(T-t)}\,dt
 =
 \begin{cases}
 \dfrac{e^{\alpha T}-e^{\beta T}}{\alpha-\beta},&\alpha\ne\beta,\\[5pt]
 Te^{\alpha T},&\alpha=\beta.
 \end{cases}
\]
Then
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \bar T_x^{AB}
 =\frac{1}{Z_{AB}^{(R)}}
 \sum_{\boldsymbol r,\boldsymbol s}
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(A)\Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(x)
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol s}(x)\Psi_{\boldsymbol s}(B)
 K_T(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol r},\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}) \;}
\]
and, for \(a=x_i\),
\[
 \boxed{\;
 \bar N_{x,i:b}^{AB}
 =\frac{s_{ab}\sqrt{\rho_a\rho_b}}{Z_{AB}^{(R)}}
 \sum_{\boldsymbol r,\boldsymbol s}
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(A)\Psi_{\boldsymbol r}(x)
 \Psi_{\boldsymbol s}(x^{i:b})\Psi_{\boldsymbol s}(B)
 K_T(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol r},\Lambda_{\boldsymbol s}) \;.}
\]

Thus the ELBO is obtained by inserting these two expressions above.  Identical
site generators create substantial spectral degeneracy:
\(\Lambda_{\boldsymbol r}\) depends only on the mode counts
\(n_k=\#\{i:r_i=k\}\),
\[
 \Lambda_{\boldsymbol r}=\sum_{k=0}^{19}n_k\lambda_k,\qquad \sum_k n_k=L.
\]
Hence the divided-difference kernel has at most
\(\binom{L+19}{19}\) distinct product eigenvalue sums (generically), rather
than \(20^L\).  The kernel values can therefore be grouped by eigenvalue sum,
while the full labelled endpoints and Potts interactions remain in the
corresponding grouped spectral coefficients.  For multiple endpoint intervals,
sum \(\mathcal L_{AB}\) over intervals.
